# Task 6: findings and failure analysis (July 2023 hike)

Numbers come from `report.md` (generated by `python evaluate.py score`). Real side = 628 opinionated reactions from
618 authors in r/spotify + r/truespotify, 24 Jul to 7 Aug 2023. Sim side = 26 personas x 5 repetitions per condition.

## Headline

Personas built from pre-event posts reproduce **what the community talked about** (theme rank correlation 0.82,
all 5 of the real top-5 themes) and **how many said they would leave** (13% firm leave intent vs 12% real), clearly
better than a plain prompt (0.69, 3/5, 30%). They get the **mood too negative**: they miss the defenders.

| | Personas + retrieval | Personas, no retrieval | Plain prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closest on actions (TVD, lower is better) | **0.19** | **0.19** | 0.30 |
| Closest on sentiment (TVD) | 0.17 | **0.16** | 0.19 |
| Theme rank correlation | **0.82** | 0.76 | 0.69 |
| Firm leave intent (real 12%) | **13%** | 15% | 30% |

None of the conditions reaches the real-data noise floor (action 0.06, sentiment 0.05), so there is a real gap left.

## What worked

1. **Personas beat the plain prompt on actions.** The plain prompt writes a stereotyped "internet reacts" crowd:
   lots of cancelling, downgrading, "can't afford it" and piracy (affordability 17% vs 3% real, piracy 5% vs 1%).
2. **Leave intent matches once threats are scored the same way.** Real labelers counted "if they do X I'll leave"
   as complaining. Applying that rule to simulated comments brings personas to 13-15% against 12% real.
3. **Theme ranking.** Competitor comparison, "still good value", missing lossless and greed are the top real
   themes, and the personas rank them similarly. Missing lossless is almost exactly right (12% vs 14%).
4. **No hard hindsight leakage.** No simulated comment mentions the stock move, the Twitter backlash, or later prices.

## Where it failed, and why

1. **Too few defenders (accept 22% vs 40%; positive 8% vs 24%).** The biggest miss. Pre-event r/spotify talk was
   dominated by the 2023 UI redesign, so the profiles are grievance-heavy (pain points are a required profile field;
   "likes" are secondary). In the real hike threads, satisfied subscribers showed up to say "$1 after 12 years is
   fine". People who are happy rarely post until something provokes them, so they are under-represented in the
   pre-event data the personas learn from. Segment 4 (music/genre talk, 19% of the community) was also left out,
   and those listeners are likely the least price-sensitive.
2. **Grievance carry-over (app/UI theme 50% vs 16% real).** Personas fold their standing complaint into every
   reaction ("raising the price while shuffle is still broken"). Real users did this too, but a third as often.
   All five UI-update resisters raised it in every repetition. The profile's pain points act as an anchor.
3. **Narrow switch targets.** Personas only name the services in their own profiles (Apple Music, Tidal). Real
   switchers also named Amazon Music and YouTube Music, and the plain prompt got that spread better.
4. **Retrieval adds little.** With and without retrieval are within one repetition-sd on actions and sentiment;
   retrieval helps a little on themes (0.82 vs 0.76, 5/5 vs 4/5) and on "inflation expected" (9% vs 3% real 11%),
   because the retrieved posts include users' own earlier price talk. The profile already carries most of the signal.
5. **Equal weighting.** Every persona counts the same, but segments differ in size and in how much they post.
   The podcast segment (2 personas) has the highest simulated leave rate (60%), which pushes totals up.

## Hindsight check

About 4-5% of persona comments (0% of plain-prompt comments) say "another price hike" or "raising it again",
although the announcement says this is the first US increase. That is either generic phrasing or a trace of the
model knowing about the later 2024/2026 hikes. It is small, does not change any metric, and is listed comment by
comment in `report.md`. The 2026 holdout is the stronger test, since that outcome is recent.

## Caveats to state in the report

- **One metric choice was made after seeing results:** scoring sentiment/themes on opinionated real rows only
  (instead of all relevant rows, a third of which are questions/information) and the "firm leave intent" rule.
  Both make the comparison like-for-like and both versions are reported; they are frozen now for the 2026 holdout.
- Labels come from Haiku, the same model family as the simulator. A 100-row audit by a second model agreed on about
  97% of relevance, sentiment and action calls; `handcheck_100.csv` is ready for a human check.
- Ground truth is Reddit posters only, front-loaded (60% of rows in the first two days).
- Within a repetition, one agent wrote all 26 personas, so personas may be more alike than independent calls would be.

## What to try before freezing (allowed: 2023 is the tuning event)

1. Add a "satisfied subscriber" persona per segment, or rebalance profiles so likes carry as much weight as pain points.
2. Weight personas by segment size when aggregating.
3. Then freeze and run the 2026 holdout once.

## Tuning round (v2, run once, then frozen)

Change, decided before re-running: a satisfied-subscriber persona in segments with >= 50 satisfied pre-event rows that
mention Spotify/Premium (Premium value judges, Disillusioned comparers; the other three fell below the bar), and every
profile redrafted so reasons to stay carry the same weight as pain points (28 personas, `personas_v2/`). Simulation
instructions unchanged.

| | Tuned + retrieval | Tuned, no retrieval | v1 + retrieval | Plain prompt | Real |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Action TVD | 0.11 | **0.08** | 0.19 | 0.30 | noise floor 0.06 |
| Sentiment TVD | 0.20 | **0.14** | 0.17 | 0.19 | noise floor 0.05 |
| Theme rank correlation | 0.88 | **0.89** | 0.82 | 0.69 | |
| Accept / defend | 32% | 35% | 22% | 28% | 40% |
| Leave rate (all / firm) | 14% / 10% | 13% / 9% | 25% / 13% | 35% / 30% | 12% |

- **Fixed:** the defender gap mostly closed (accept 22% -> 32-35% vs 40%), raw leave intent now matches without any
  re-scoring (13-14% vs 12%), action distance halved and is close to the noise floor (0.08 vs 0.06).
- **Not fixed:** grievance carry-over (app/UI theme still 47-50% vs 16%), and defenders sound neutral rather than
  positive (positive 7-10% vs 24%). "Still good value" now overshoots (39-42% vs 21%).
- **Retrieval no longer helps:** with the rebalanced profiles, the no-retrieval condition is as good or better on
  every metric. The retrieved posts are mostly complaints, so they pull personas back toward the grievance voice.
- Hindsight cue rate unchanged (4-5% "another hike" phrasing), no hard leakage.

**Frozen for the 2026 holdout:** v2 persona recipe (`persona_config_v2.json`, `make_personas.py`), simulation
instructions (`simulate.py`), label sets and labeling brief (`evaluate.py`), scoring rules (`score.py`, opinion rows,
firm-leave rule). Primary method remains "personas + retrieval" as pre-specified; the no-retrieval and plain
conditions are reported alongside.

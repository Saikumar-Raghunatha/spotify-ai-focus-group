What would r/spotify say about a price hike?

We built 28 AI personas from what Spotify users posted before each price increase, showed them the announcement, and compared their answers with what the real community said afterwards.

How it was built

  • Data: Reddit r/spotify and r/truespotify, pulled from the Arctic Shift archive. Usernames were replaced by hashes.
  • No peeking: personas only use posts written before the news leaked (WSJ, 21 Jul 2023; Financial Times, ~25 Nov 2025).
  • Segments: posts were embedded with a sentence model and grouped into six segments with k-means; five are simulated.
  • Personas: Claude Haiku wrote each profile from ~40 real posts; each persona keeps its own searchable post history.
  • Scoring: real comments were labeled with the same actions, sentiments and themes as the AI answers.

Glossary

Say they'll leave
Share of answers that say they will cancel, switch to another service, or move to a cheaper plan.
Defend the price
Share that accept the increase or argue it is fair.
Action gap
How different two action mixes are, from 0 (identical) to 1 (nothing in common). Technically: total variation distance.
Noise floor
The gap between two random halves of the real data (about 0.06–0.07). No simulation can be expected to do better.
Plain prompt
The AI is asked to imagine 28 Reddit users, with no real data. This is what most people would try first.
Tuning round
One change made after the 2023 test: a satisfied-subscriber persona in two segments, and reasons-to-stay weighted like complaints. Then everything was frozen for 2026.

Limits

Reddit posters are not all customers. Personas still raise app complaints and plan prices more than real users do, and under-count people who defend the price. The same model family writes and labels the answers. Treat this as an early signal for comparing scenarios, not a churn forecast.
